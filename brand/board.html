1Lanes

Many sessions run in parallel. One of them waits on you.

Three rounded bars are agent sessions, a list or a timeline seen from above. The middle one is shorter and ends in an amber dot: the session that stopped on a question. It shows the product in one look, with no letter and no metaphor to explain. At 16 px the bars are 2 px tall with 1.4 px gaps, the dot is 3 px, and it still reads as three lines and a point.

2Meter

An instrument: what it costs, and whether you can relax.

An open dial with the needle resting low, the calm reading. The opening at the foot holds a small amber dot for what waits on you. It says measurement and cost, the part of Teyla people pay attention to first. At 16 px the ring is a 1.75 px stroke and the needle a short diagonal. The amber dot drops to under 2 px and fades, so the mark leans on its silhouette.

3Cursor

The CLI's own letter: a T whose right arm is a waiting cursor.

A heavy T built from blocks. Its right arm is a separate amber block, the cursor a terminal shows when it waits for input. It is a monogram that belongs to a command-line tool, and it carries the "waiting on you" idea without a bubble. At 16 px it is a solid T plus one 2.5 by 4 px block. It is the most legible of the three and works as a one-character banner in the terminal.

In use: concept 3, Cursor

Cursor is the strongest mark: at 16 px it is still a solid T and one block, and a T followed by a cursor block is a two-character terminal banner. Its amber block says "waiting on you" the way developers already read it, a cursor waiting for input. Lanes turns into a hamburger-menu icon at small sizes, and Meter reads as a generic speedometer.

Concepts 1 and 2 share the round cut of the wordmark; Cursor uses the block cut, fitted to its bars. Type: -apple-system for UI, ui-monospace for code and the terminal.
