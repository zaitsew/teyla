# Playbook and Watch: best practices that reach the product and the customer

Status: plan, 2026-11-28, after one research round and one critique round with Grok 4.7 and GPT-6.1 Sol.
Decisions P1–P12. Extends [Studio](../STUDIO.md); replaces nothing in it except the design scout's proposals (P9).

## 1. The finding: the tools exist, nothing flows through them

Measured 2026-11-28:

| Piece | Built | Used |
|---|---|---|
| Taste corpus (`teyla taste add`, phone share sheet) | yes, Studio 2 | **0 entries** |
| Design scout (`teyla scout design`, 14 feeds, weekly) | yes | 4 weekly files; 1 proposal ever, undrafted; 0 PRs |
| Reference library (`templates/design/REFERENCE.md`) | 7 screen types | 3 entries still "to confirm" |
| Hook library, swipe file, competitor list | none | the pilot product has no competitor doc |
| Growth factory | yes | 1,317 items; no pattern, audience or offer behind them |

The gap is not a missing fetcher. Three links are missing: (a) nobody shows the owner real material in a form he
answers in one tap; (b) observations never become reusable, citable patterns; (c) lanes never get the right pattern
when they build a screen, write a string or draft a post. And the system has no path from a better product to a
customer. The plan builds those links, proves them by hand on the pilot product, and automates only what the owner actually uses.

## 2. Decisions

- **P1. Part of Teyla Studio; the content is Markdown.** "Teyla or mds": both. Teyla gets `teyla playbook` and
  `teyla watch`; the library is Markdown cards with YAML front matter in a private repo, reviewed like code.
  No new product, no new repo.
- **P2. Where things live.** Engine and schema: the Teyla repo (public): `src/teyla/playbook.py`,
  `src/teyla/watch.py`, `templates/playbook/card.schema.json`. Cards: the private ops repo `playbook/cards/<domain>/<id>.md`
  (private: competitor notes and our strategy). Per product: `<repo>/docs/watch.toml`. Watch log and media:
  `~/.teyla/watch/`, never in git. Paid-library images never enter git.
- **P3. Seven domains.** `interaction` (gestures, swipe actions, context menus, haptics, motion, sheets, widgets,
  Live Activities, App Intents), `flow` (first run, permissions, empty and error states, paywall, cancel, settings),
  `ai` (companion turns, generative UI, voice, oversight, undo, memory, uncertainty), `visual` (layout, type,
  colour, density, data viz), `writing` (microcopy, naming, errors, notifications), `listing` (store page,
  screenshots, preview, ASO, landing, README), `demand` (audience, offer, hooks, 7–15 s video, posts, launch,
  trial length). Accessibility stays in the UX standard.
- **P4. One unit, the card, filled in stages.** An agent writes only what it saw. A person adds judgement later.
  - *Intake (`seen`), agent-filled:* `id`, `domain`, `kind` (`pattern` | `hook`), `job` (the user's job, ≤8 words),
    `surface`, `platform`, `mechanism` (one transferable sentence, no brand), `refuse` (skin, phrase or brand we
    will not copy), `evidence[]` (`product`, `url`, `seen` date, `what` one sentence, optional local `media`,
    `observed` true | `unknown` for what public media cannot show), `added_by`, `created`. Hooks add `line`,
    `format`, `promise`, `proof`, `cta`. Body ≤150 words.
  - *Candidate:* `problem` (`product`, `text`, `pointer` to a capture, decision, review, growth item or the owner's
    words). `severity` 1–3 and `confidence` low | medium | high are set by the orchestrator and shown to the owner;
    no 0–6 score.
  - *Trial (the owner's yes):* `trial` = `hypothesis`, `metric`, `baseline`, `threshold` with direction,
    `window_days`, `guardrails`, `review_by`, two `variants` (drawings), `approved` date.
  - *Closed:* `verdict` = `adopted` | `rejected` | `inconclusive` | `parked` (with a dated trigger), numbers, PR.
    Hooks add `results[]` that point at the growth item id in `~/.teyla/growth/` with its denominator.
  `teyla playbook check` enforces exactly the fields of the card's stage, so an agent cannot invent a baseline to pass.
- **P5. Status and expiry.** `seen` → `candidate` → `trial` → closed. `seen` expires at 90 days, `candidate` at
  30 days; the evidence stays in the file. A card becomes `candidate` only with a problem already observed in a
  product; "looks current" is not a problem. A card becomes a STANDARD rule only when every future screen must
  obey it, inside the 20-rule cap.
- **P6. Caps.** One trial a week per product; two a week across the studio. Copy the mechanism, never the skin.
  Briefs pass mechanisms and refusals, never "make it like <brand>".
- **P7. Retrieval at the task.** `teyla playbook brief --product P --surface S [--domain D]` returns ≤5 cards,
  ≤1,500 tokens, only `candidate | trial | adopted`; "none fit" is a valid answer. Wired into `teyla copy brief`
  and `teyla growth draft`; design lanes call it by hand. The artifact (PR, draft, string table) names the card
  ids it used or "none fit". The library is never pasted whole or loaded at session start.
- **P8. Watch: three rings, ≤12 per product.** `job` (≤8, same job and platform, one indie we respect), `craft`
  (≤4, the best interaction next door), `platform` (HIG What's New, App Store editorial: the scout's existing
  feeds). Plus one studio-wide `frontier` list of ≤8 AI and big-tech products. Discovery from beyond-intelligence
  launches and from names users mention in reviews; never from follower counts. Stable exemplars stay even in a
  quiet quarter; the owner culls quarterly.
- **P9. Watch is a log; cards come from teardowns and taps.** Weekly and automatic, free sources only: App Store
  Lookup (version, release notes, description, screenshot set) per storefront, diffed and deduplicated; App Store
  review RSS sampled across ratings, quotes kept only when they name the job. It writes `~/.teyla/watch/` and
  **mints no card**. Cards come from (a) the monthly teardown lane, one rotating journey, marking what public media
  cannot show as `unknown`, and (b) the owner's taps. Page hash diffs are dropped. The design scout keeps its weekly
  reading file and stops writing proposals: one weekly interrupt, not two.
- **P10. The owner loop: one Friday digest, run by hand for three Fridays first.** At most five items across the
  studio. Each item shows the artifact itself (a screenshot or the exact line), the source, the mechanism and the
  refusal. Two separate answers: *taste* (Like / Dislike / Skip, with an optional sentence; it writes a taste-corpus
  entry with the image) and *Try* (shown only on a candidate; it displays the hypothesis, metric, scope and two
  drawings before the owner approves). Silence is unanswered, not a no. Delivery for the pilot: one private Artifact
  page with buttons that store the answers; the Friday session imports them. Automation (`teyla tg` cards or the
  Mac app) is built only if the owner answers two of the three pilot Fridays.
- **P11. Customers: one audience, one offer, one channel, one funnel.** Per product, `demand` starts with a
  positioning card: the audience, the trigger moment, the offer, the channel and an attributable CTA. Results are
  counted as qualified visits → installs → first useful plan → return, with denominators from the existing records
  (usage probe, store analytics, growth ledger). A pilot always runs one acquisition or positioning trial beside
  one product trial. Saves and replies are signals, not customers.
- **P12. Money.** A $0 pilot. Paid libraries are a later §11 question with prices: Mobbin Pro $120/yr (MCP,
  internal use only), ScreensDesign $129/yr (sale ends 30 Nov), Page Flows $99/yr, Appfigures from $9.99/mo.
  X reads stay off. Codex votes $0; Grok votes Mobbin Pro for the iOS pilot. Default: $0 until manual reference
  gathering costs real time on two separate tasks.

About 12 cards in total, all at `seen`. One PR per lane into the private ops repo; each lane writes only its own files.

### Phase C — the the pilot product pilot, by hand (next 3 weeks)

1. the owner picks the positioning default from B1 (one question, one default).
2. The orchestrator promotes the cards that meet a named the pilot product problem to `candidate`, with severity and confidence.
3. Friday digest #1 on **2026-12-04**: ≤5 items as an Artifact page; taste and Try answered separately.
4. At most one product trial and one acquisition trial, each with the P4 trial fields and two drawings. A normal
   the pilot product lane builds the approved product trial; the acquisition trial runs in the existing daily slots.
5. Digests #2 and #3 on 12-11 and 12-18; verdicts at each trial's `review_by`.

Exit: two of three digests answered on artifacts; one real artifact (a screen PR, the store text or a post) that
cites card ids from a `brief`; one trial with a verdict and numbers. Then the digest is automated.

### Phase D — every product

Only after the Phase C exit. Products join one at a time with a `docs/watch.toml`; the Friday routine runs
`teyla watch` and the monthly teardown; the owner culls watch lists quarterly.

## 4. Deliberately not built

No embeddings or vector store (exact tags first). No page hash diffs. No automatic cards from the watch. No
scraping of Mobbin, Refero, Meta or TikTok libraries; no X ingestion. No automatic rule promotion. No new
dashboard. No digest automation before the owner has answered by hand. No inspiration domain: a screen without a job
and a refusal line is Dribbble.

## 5. Risks

1. **A museum.** Mitigation: staged cards, expiry, one weekly interrupt, the Phase C exit counts answers and verdicts.
2. **Invented evidence.** Mitigation: `check` refuses fields beyond the stage; `unknown` is a valid observation.
3. **Same face.** Mitigation: mechanisms and refusals in briefs, never brands; a product's tokens and voice win.
4. **Content without customers.** Mitigation: P11; the acquisition trial is measured to installs and first plan.
5. **ToS and copyright.** Mitigation: links and own words in git; media local; official APIs; no tracing.
6. **The machine and the bill.** No simulator or build in Phases A–B; lanes respect `teyla load`; Grok lanes report
   `teyla grok-cost`.
